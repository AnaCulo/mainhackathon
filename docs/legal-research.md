# Legal research for a Dutch whistleblower intake agent

**Short version:** [one-page legal summary](legal-summary.md).

**Assessment date: 20 November 2026.** This review covers an employer's internal reporting procedure in the Netherlands. The hackathon tests fictional reports. The deployment analysis below assumes a Dutch employer chooses the purposes, service providers and recipients. No employer mandate, sector, executed processor contract, production configuration or test result was supplied.

## A. Executive summary

The agent can help a reporter put events in order, register receipt and prepare an unverified draft. A designated, independent person must be able to see the necessary evidence, correct the draft, follow up and communicate with the reporter. The proposed workflow reserves investigation, disclosure, escalation and closure for that person. Losing the connection to a model must never make a received report disappear or restart its statutory clock. [Directive, Arts. 8–9, 16–18][dir] · [Wbk, Arts. 1a, 2–2b][wbk]

For this MVP, use synthetic text, keep identity mappings local, and block live caller audio to ElevenLabs. A person can be identified by their shift, voice or evidence even after name removal. BRANE sits downstream of the voice provider. No basis has been established for disclosing real, unfiltered speech to that provider. These are prototype design decisions. The [policy matrix](policy-matrix.md) gives the release rules, and the [voice scripts](voice-disclosure.md) specify when a caller can proceed.

## B. What does the EU Whistleblower Directive require?

**Who must have a channel?** Article 8 covers public entities and private entities with at least 50 workers, subject to the Directive's smaller-entity sector rules and national options. Private entities with 50–249 workers may share reception and investigation resources while remaining responsible for confidentiality, feedback and addressing the breach. Outsourcing reception preserves the safeguards. Article 9(2) permits written or oral reporting, or both. Where oral reporting is offered, it provides for telephone or another voice system and, on request, a physical meeting within a reasonable period. An AI voice provider is a technology choice. The Dutch procedure below gives reporters a wider choice of routes. [Directive, Arts. 8(1–9), 9(1–2)][dir]

**When do the internal clocks start?** Acknowledge within seven days of receipt. Give feedback within a reasonable period, at most three months from acknowledgement. If none was sent, count three months from expiry of the seven-day period after reporting. Feedback communicates planned or taken follow-up and the reasons. A pending investigation still needs a substantive update. The deadlines for an external authority under Article 11, including its possible six-month period, do not govern this employer's internal channel. Reporters may go directly to a competent authority. [Directive, Arts. 5(13), 9(1)(b–g), 10, 11(2)][dir]

**How should n8n calculate them?** Timestamp actual receipt in the protected register, save acknowledgement dispatch separately, and use Europe/Amsterdam calendar dates. Exclude the trigger day when counting seven days and use calendar months, with month-end handling when the corresponding date is absent. EU and Dutch time-limit rules address weekends and public holidays. For operations, set reminders and send by the unextended date, earlier where needed. An extension requires a checked legal calendar. A demo clock must never overwrite the actual dates. [EU time limits, Art. 3][time] · [Dutch Algemene termijnenwet, Arts. 1–4][atw]

| Internal example | Acknowledgement | Feedback target |
|---|---|---|
| Received Friday 20 Nov 2026 and acknowledged Monday 23 Nov | Sent before the Friday 27 Nov target | By Tuesday 23 Feb 2027 |
| Received 20 Nov, no acknowledgement sent | Flag the missed 27 Nov target | EU fallback starts at expiry of that period. Target Friday 26 Feb operationally, ahead of Saturday 27 Feb. Keep the missed-receipt flag |

**What if the report is anonymous?** Member States generally decide whether to require acceptance and follow-up of anonymous reports, subject to sector-specific EU obligations. A reporter who is later identified and faces retaliation can qualify for protection under Article 6(3). Offer a pseudonymous return mailbox where supported. Use it to acknowledge and provide feedback without demanding a name. If no safe route exists, register receipt, document the failed or impossible contact, keep the internal timer, and send the report to the independent handler. No invented address or false delivery status. The no-acknowledgement calculation is explicit in the Directive. Dutch Article 2(2)(i) describes the clock following dispatch and supplies no express no-contact exemption. **Requires legal verification** for a particular employer's treatment of an unreachable reporter. [Directive, Arts. 6(2–3), 9(1)(b–f)][dir] · [Wbk, Arts. 2, 2a][wbk]

**Who may see whose identity?** Limit reporter identity and identifying clues to authorised people competent to receive or follow up. Outside that circle, obtain explicit agreement unless disclosure is necessary and proportionate under a legal obligation in an authority investigation or judicial proceedings. Give prior notice and written reasons unless notice would jeopardise the proceedings. Protect the confidentiality of other named people, including the subject and witnesses. Preserve the subject's defence rights and presumption of innocence. A handler should distinguish “the reporter says” from a verified finding. The general staff directory, HR inbox and model provider have no automatic entitlement to identities. [Directive, Arts. 9(1)(a), 16, 22][dir] · [Wbk, Art. 1a][wbk]

**What is the record of an oral report?** Under Article 18(2), for a recorded line with the reporter's consent, use a retrievable durable recording or a full, accurate transcript. Offer transcript review, correction and signature. For an unrecorded line the responsible staff member may write accurate minutes and must offer the same opportunity. Meetings under Article 18(4) have specific consent and record requirements. A model-generated synopsis alone cannot serve as a complete oral record. Article 17 requires compliance with data-protection law and prompt removal of manifestly irrelevant personal data collected inadvertently. Retain reports only as necessary and proportionate. AI drafting changes the recipients and risks, while the employer retains its procedural duties. [Directive, Arts. 8(5), 17, 18(1–4)][dir]

## C. Which Dutch rules govern an employer's internal procedure?

| Question | Dutch position and consequence |
|---|---|
| Which employers and workers count? | An employer ordinarily employing at least 50 statutory employees must establish an internal procedure. Count persons working under an employment contract, public appointment, or otherwise in a subordinate relationship for remuneration. Check the facts for paid trainees and agency arrangements. The threshold falls away for employers within the EU instruments listed in Annex Parts I.B and II, including covered financial-sector entities. Identify the specific instrument. Private employers with 50–249 and specified municipal bodies may share resources. The smaller private employer transition ended 17 Dec 2023. [Wbk, Arts. 1, 2(1), (3–4), 21c][wbk] · [Huis, employer guidance][huis] |
| Which reports qualify? | The definition covers specified breaches or risks of breach of Union law and defined public-interest wrongdoing. For the domestic limb, an individual grievance alone generally falls outside the definition. A pattern, structural issue, serious or extensive conduct can matter. Reasonable grounds based on work-related knowledge matter. Let a human assess borderline cases rather than rejecting a report at intake. [Wbk, Art. 1, definitions of misstand and vermoeden van een misstand][wbk] |
| Which routes and rights? | Set out written, telephone or other voice, and timely in-person reporting on request. Designate independent reception and follow-up officials, allow confidential consultation with an adviser, and give information about external reporting and protection. Assess staff participation when adopting the procedure. Retaliation is prohibited under the statutory conditions, with a rebuttable presumption linking detriment to reporting. Direct external reporting is possible. [Wbk, Arts. 2(2)(c,d,g), 2(5–7), 17e, 17eb][wbk] · [Huis, questions 7–9][huis] |
| Must anonymous reports be accepted? | The national consolidated text explicitly marks Art. 2(2)(e) and (f) as **not in force** on the assessment date. The 2023 commencement order excluded them. The government later chose to address anonymity in a future bill. Offer name-free reporting as a design decision, check sector-specific rules, and disclose limits of identifiability. [Wbk, Art. 2(2)][wbk] · [Commencement order][start] · [Ministry update, anonymous reporting][anon] |
| How is follow-up documented? | Register the report on receipt. Send acknowledgement within seven days and communicate the assessment and applicable follow-up within a reasonable period of at most three months after dispatch of acknowledgement. Destroy register data when no longer needed for applicable legal requirements. For oral reporting, prior reporter agreement is required for recording. The alternative is a complete and accurate written account with an opportunity to check, correct and sign. [Wbk, Arts. 2(2)(h–i), 2a, 2b][wbk] |
| What is confidential? | Everyone handling the report or investigation must protect confidential information, including identities, traceable details and trade secrets. Statutory and duty-based necessities in Art. 1a(1) coexist with the particular protections for reporter identity in (3–5). Route legal disclosure to the responsible human, document necessity, recipient and notice. Check sector-specific procedures under Art. 1b. [Wbk, Arts. 1a–1b][wbk] |

## D. Which GDPR conditions apply to each operation?

**Who determines the purpose?** The deploying employer ordinarily decides why a report is gathered and who receives it. Record who makes these decisions in the actual arrangement. A supplier acting on documented instructions may be a processor or subprocessor. A supplier determining its own purposes may be a separate controller for those operations. Joint determination needs separate analysis. A hosted tool, contract label or hackathon credit establishes none of these facts. Genuinely fictional fixtures outside any identifiable person's context differ from a real speaker, caller ID, IP address or service account log. [GDPR, recital 26 and Arts. 4(1), (7–8), 24, 26, 28(10)][gdpr]

| Operation | Candidate legal basis and limiting condition |
|---|---|
| Receive, register, follow up, acknowledge and provide feedback | For a covered employer, Art. 6(1)(c) with 6(3) and the applicable Wbk duty can support processing **necessary** for that duty. Identify the obligation, data and recipient. This does not automatically authorise optional AI, recordings or analytics. [GDPR, Art. 6][gdpr] · [Wbk, Arts. 2–2b][wbk] |
| Voluntary channel outside a statutory duty | A private employer might establish a legitimate interest under 6(1)(f). Document purpose, necessity, alternatives, reasonable expectations and balancing, including retaliation risks. A public authority performing its tasks cannot use (f) for those tasks. A public-interest task under (e) needs a legal foundation. If no basis fits, do not process real reports by this route. [GDPR, Art. 6(1), (3), recitals 45, 47][gdpr] |
| Transmit speech, transcribe, record, retain or send to a model | Assess each purpose and recipient separately. The Dutch obligation to provide oral reporting does not prescribe an external speech service. Permission to record under Wbk 2b(2) is distinct from a GDPR Art. 6 basis, Art. 9 condition or Ch. V transfer mechanism. If relying on GDPR consent, assess freedom to refuse and withdraw, specificity and the employment power imbalance. The caller cannot consent for everyone mentioned. [GDPR, Arts. 4(11), 6, 7, 9, recital 43][gdpr] · [Wbk, Art. 2b][wbk] |
| Process health, ethnicity, trade-union membership or other special categories | Require both an Art. 6 basis and a specific Art. 9(2) exception. Potential examples include necessity for a legal claim under (f), or substantial public interest under (g) grounded in law with safeguards. Neither covers every report. Raw voice can identify someone and is not automatically biometric data used for unique identification under Art. 9. Exclude medical facts from external AI in this MVP even if local processing could be lawful. Wbk 1c grants specified powers to the Huis itself. [GDPR, Arts. 4(14), 9][gdpr] · [Wbk, Art. 1c][wbk] |
| Handle allegations of criminal conduct | Art. 10 requires official authority or EU/member-state authorisation with safeguards in addition to Art. 6. A vague process anomaly differs from a concrete accusation about an identifiable person. Flag uncertainty locally, preserve allegation status, avoid any guilt label. Under UAVG 33(2)(b) certain processing for the controller's own protection is limited to offences against it or its staff. Art. 33(3) imposes conditions concerning rules for processing personnel data of this kind. Check Art. 32 and other specific routes against the actual facts. No general employer permission follows from Wbk 1c(2). **Requires legal verification** before retaining or disclosing a real allegation. [GDPR, Arts. 5(1)(d), 10][gdpr] · [UAVG, Arts. 32–33][uavg] |

**What is necessary?** For case preparation start with an opaque case reference, broad event category, approximate timeline, alleged facts and a local evidence reference. Keep identities, safe contact routes, raw evidence, original oral records and mapping keys separate and access-controlled if necessary and lawful. Case JSON can contain case-scoped tokens, a neutral draft and receipt time. Exclude unsupported inferences, unique job/shift combinations and sensitive facts. Delete inadvertently collected manifestly irrelevant data promptly. These boundaries implement purpose limitation, accuracy, minimisation, security and accountable defaults. The statutes do not mandate SQLite or universal localisation. [GDPR, Arts. 5, 24–25, 32][gdpr] · [Directive, Arts. 16–18][dir]

**What rights do reporters and named people have?** Provide accessible Art. 13 information when collecting from the reporter, including controller, purposes, basis, recipients, retention criteria, transfers, rights and complaint route. For indirect collection from named people, apply Art. 14, ordinarily within a reasonable period up to one month, earlier on first contact or disclosure where applicable. Assess specific exceptions, including Art. 14(5)(b) or statutory confidentiality under (d), case by case. Art. 15 access and copies require protection of others' rights under 15(4). Redact before withholding a whole record. Art. 23 requires a legislative measure and necessary, proportionate restrictions. UAVG 41 governs specified rights and duties, including protection of others. Record precise grounds, scope, duration, safeguards and review date. No blanket secrecy period or automatic disclosure to the accused. [GDPR, Arts. 12–15, 23][gdpr] · [UAVG, Art. 41][uavg] · [Wbk, Art. 1a][wbk]

**When does retention end?** The employer needs a per-category schedule and a decision owner. Review at intake, after follow-up, at closure and on expiry of documented legal holds. Remove irrelevant details promptly, restrict retained evidence, expire contact handles, mappings, vendor copies, execution histories and backups on documented schedules. Keep proof of deletion without copying the report into the proof. A seven-day or three-month reporting deadline is not a retention period. A single statutory number of retention years for every item is **not established from verified sources**. [Directive, Arts. 17–18][dir] · [Wbk, Art. 2a(2)][wbk] · [GDPR, Arts. 5(1)(e), 17(3), 28(3)(g)][gdpr]

**What governance is needed before live use?** Document the processing inventory under Art. 30 as applicable, including purposes, data subjects, recipients, transfers, retention and safeguards. Design role-based access, separation of duties, protected backups, testing and content-free logs against the risk under Arts. 25 and 32. Establish incident escalation. A processor informs the controller without undue delay. The controller notifies the supervisory authority without undue delay and, where feasible, within 72 hours of awareness unless risk is unlikely. Document breaches and assess the distinct high-risk communication duty to individuals. [GDPR, Arts. 24–25, 28, 30, 32–34][gdpr]

**Is a DPIA mandatory?** Assess likelihood of high risk before personal-data processing under Art. 35(1). Art. 35(3) expressly includes certain systematic evaluations with significant effects and large-scale Art. 9 or 10 processing. The AP's Art. 35(4) list includes defined covert investigations, fraud prevention and worker monitoring. Check their precise conditions. An isolated synthetic intake test does not establish these conditions. For deployment, employee vulnerability, retaliation, audio outsourcing and sensitive narratives together warrant a documented DPIA assessment. Scope the whole chain, document necessity, proportionality, safeguards and residual risk, consult the DPO where designated, and consult the AP before processing if high residual risk remains. [GDPR, Arts. 35–36][gdpr] · [AP mandatory DPIA list, preamble and items 1, 3, 7, 11][dpia]

## E. How does the EU AI Act apply on 20 November 2026?

**Purpose and scope.** The intended function is receiving a narrative and drafting a case for a human. No safety component of a regulated product is apparent. No recruitment, employee evaluation, credibility assessment or consequential ranking is specified. On those facts an Annex III purpose has not been identified. An internal intake tool used by an employer does not automatically perform the employment functions in Annex III point 4. A pre-market research/testing exercise may fall within Art. 2(8), which excludes testing in real-world conditions from that carve-out. Distinguish the team's prototype from already marketed upstream systems and from a live pilot. [AI Act, Art. 2(8)][aiscope] · [Art. 6(1–3)][ai6] · [Annex III, in particular 4(b)][annex]

**What would change the assessment?** Monitoring an employee's conduct, scoring credibility, ranking allegations in a way that controls access to investigation, or recommending discipline or dismissal may materially influence employment decisions. Examine intended purpose, effects and actual use, including reliance on the recommendation. Simple deadline ordering differs from ranking people by risk. If an Annex III use is identified, examine Art. 6(3)'s significant-risk test and specific exceptions, including narrow procedural or preparatory tasks. Profiling natural persons within an Annex III use precludes that exception. A human click does not remove the classification. Require a fresh assessment before expanding scope. [AI Act, Art. 6(2–4)][ai6] · [Annex III, point 4][annex]

**Which dates matter?** The 2026 Digital Omnibus amended the original timetable. Article 50 generally applies from **2 August 2026**. Chapter III Sections 1–3, except Art. 6(5), apply to Annex III high-risk systems from **2 December 2027** and to Annex I product systems from **2 August 2028**. For providers of synthetic-content systems already placed on the market before 2 August 2026, amended Art. 111(4) sets **2 December 2026** for compliance with Art. 50(2). This narrow transition does not postpone interaction disclosure. Verify which entity is provider or deployer and when its system was placed on the market. [Reg. 2026/1744, Art. 1(39–40)][omnibus] · [Commission implementation timeline][timeline]

**What must the caller hear?** Under Art. 50(1), providers of interactive AI must design for disclosure unless interaction with AI is obvious to a reasonably informed, observant and circumspect person, subject to the statutory exception. Disclose clearly at first contact and make the information accessible under 50(5). State that the voice is synthetic. Separately, 50(2) addresses **provider-side machine-readable marking and detectability** for generated content, subject to technical feasibility and statutory exceptions. Spoken disclosure cannot establish that marking. Art. 50(4) addresses deployer disclosure of content constituting a deep fake. A generic artificial voice need not imitate an existing person or meet that definition. Check the voice, provenance and provider evidence before real use. [AI Act, Art. 50(1–5)][ai50] · [2026 amendment][omnibus]

## F. Why keep mappings separate and insist on meaningful human review?

The controller can re-identify case tokens through its mapping. A recipient may infer identities from role, shift or incident details. Use a different mapping per case, separate privileges and keys, and test whether combinations single someone out. The EDPB's 01/2025 consultation text explains attribution and separation. In *EDPS v SRB*, interpreting the parallel EU-institutions regulation, the Court distinguished identifiability for different recipients and assessed the controller's information duty from its own perspective at collection. Tokenisation alone gives no basis here for treating external case data as anonymous. [GDPR, Art. 4(5), recital 26][gdpr] · [EDPB 01/2025, paras. 16–22, 59–61][pseudo] · [CJEU C-413/23 P, paras. 73–86, 100–106][srb]

GDPR Art. 22(1) concerns solely automated decisions with legal or similarly significant effects. Its exceptions and safeguards in (2–4) need specific justification. Drafting and administrative routing can proceed automatically within the matrix. Under P6 the model may never decide whether an allegation is true or what should happen to a person. A reviewer must have the source evidence, competence, time and authority to disagree and change the outcome. Record the person's reasoning. Employment-related profiling also raises fairness and accuracy concerns even before a final decision. [GDPR, Arts. 4(4), 5, 22][gdpr] · [WP251rev.01, §IV.A, pp. 20–21][wp] · [Dutch AP, human intervention framework][human]
