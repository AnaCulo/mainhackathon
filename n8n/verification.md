## Integration decisions

The Node sender’s P5 ALLOW and P6 HUMAN both have count 1. Its additional acknowledgement is a draft and is discarded. Its protected brief is allowlisted and remains in the restricted register. The sender stamps receipt before checking and inference, then saves after success. A failed first attempt can therefore lose original receipt. B must persist that clock and make held reports available locally. The Node adapter does not supply a deployed policy fingerprint or independent proof of the screened payload. D’s handoff check trusts the authenticated backend verdict. A and B must verify it for each live route.

Python 3c7dda9 reserves receipt durably before model work. Its sender uses a five-second timeout, bearer N8N_WEBHOOK_SECRET, microsecond UTC, six possible categories and P6 HUMAN_ONLY, with null counts on P5/P6. D accepts these explicit success variants. The wire case has summary and tokens; the detailed extraction stays local. Review displays the supplied summary as reported facts and identifies absent timeline/evidence. The backend requires HTTPS outside the fixture transport. Its advertised 200 response alone does not establish successful webhook delivery; inspect the outbox and register.

The approved production dispatcher, SSO/alternate-handler assignment, working employer oral/meeting channel, processor approvals and deployment review are outside the demonstrated setup. The four role scripts and synthetic case capture do not establish those production capabilities.
